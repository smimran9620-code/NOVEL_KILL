| 1 | Her disgrace is commuted to one service: certify the bridge she designed. She signs it sound so the garrison can retreat across — and the certificate is captured with the retreat's papers. | Her restored name becomes the enemy's map. |
| 2 | Because the invaders will cross at her own certified span, command orders her to rig it; only she knows the three flaws that make rigging possible. | She must write down the flaw she concealed at the inquiry — the confession that ends her for good. |
| 3 | Because a span only drops under load, she needs the crossing hour, which only the scout she loves can supply. | She tells him what she is; his silence puts his life in her hands. |
| 4 | Because his silence is noticed, the army advances the crossing and sends his troop over first. She fires with him on the span. | She kills the man she loves, and the span half-falls, leaving a walkway. |
| 5 | Because the wreck still carries men, she goes out on it at dawn to cut the last charge by hand as the column comes. | Her hands, her life — and a town that will remember her as the woman who destroyed her bridge. |
