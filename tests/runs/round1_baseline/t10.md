**Diagnosis: largely working — with one real weakness.**

The scene earns its tension through concrete behavior: the burnt sugar smell, the tray set down too hard, the rolling pin stopping. Nothing is announced; character comes through action. The mother's arithmetic speech is the scene's engine — it explains her without excusing her, and gives Ana the exact thing she can't argue with. The final exchange ("I already do") lands because it's shaped like cruelty but reads as exhausted love, and going back to the dough is a better exit than any line could be.

What's weaker:

1. **Ana's interiority is summarized, not felt.** "She found she could not, because her mother had done the arithmetic first" tells us the insight rather than letting us watch Ana reach it. Consider cutting the explanation and letting the tray's weight or her silence carry it.

2. **"Which one would you like me to choose?"** is slightly too rhetorical — a touch of self-pity the rest of the scene avoids. A shorter, flatter version would be crueler and truer.

The close is genuinely good. Keep it.
