## Commentary

The beats are right; the verbs are doing filler work. "Exploded inward" is stock — doors blow in. "Came up shooting" is good and should stay. "Grabbed him by the collar" is three words of throat-clearing for "had his collar." "Headbutted the bastard right in the teeth" is the best line here, and the profanity earns its place, so don't sand it off — but "right in" softens the hit; cut it. "Blood sprayed" is a missed chance: spray is fine, but give it a direction or a surface. "Kade grinned" lands harder as its own beat when something physical precedes it. Keep the sentence lengths lurching — short, shorter, then one that runs. Add no interiority; the register dies the moment Kade starts thinking.

## Revised

The door blew in. Kade rolled, came up shooting. Two men dropped. A third had his collar; Kade headbutted him in the teeth and felt something give. Blood sprayed across his knuckles. Kade grinned.

"You picked the wrong night," he said.
