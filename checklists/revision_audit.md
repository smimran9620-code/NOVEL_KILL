# Manuscript Revision Audit

Run at four levels, in order. Do not edit lines while structural problems remain: you will polish paragraphs that will be deleted.

## Level 1 - Scene audit

- [ ] Every scene has a change statement that is not "nothing".
- [ ] Every scene's turn is caused by a character decision.
- [ ] No scene only repeats information the reader had.
- [ ] The three most important scenes are dramatised, not summarised.
- [ ] The climax is dramatised in full.

Sample: audit 10 scenes spread across the book. If more than three fail, stop and fix scenes before anything else.

## Level 2 - Chapter audit

- [ ] Every chapter raises or presses a question.
- [ ] Every chapter ends on a decision or consequence, not a withheld fact.
- [ ] Entry points vary; no chapter opens with recap or weather.
- [ ] Causality: no beat joined by "and then".
- [ ] POV is not a passive recipient for consecutive chapters.
- [ ] Scene types rotate.

Sample: audit 10 chapters. Count change statements; any chapter with no change is merged or cut.

## Level 3 - Arc audit

- [ ] Each arc has its own question, cost, and payoff.
- [ ] Each arc ends by changing the terms of the next.
- [ ] Escalation narrows options rather than inflating power.
- [ ] Antagonists act off-page and arrive as consequences.
- [ ] Subplots converge; nothing expands without a closing chapter.
- [ ] The protagonist's character changes measurably across the arc.

## Level 4 - Whole-manuscript audit

### Promise and payoff

- [ ] Opening promise identified: ______
- [ ] Ending keeps or deliberately transforms it, and the transformation is signalled.
- [ ] Every setup in the register is paid or retired.
- [ ] The central dramatic question is answered, or its openness is the answer.

### Causality sweep

- [ ] Retell the plot as "because/therefore" from inciting decision to final consequence without gaps.
- [ ] Any place where the retelling needs "then something happened" is a causality hole.
- [ ] Coincidences: list them. Inciting incident and complication are acceptable; resolution is not.

### Repetition sweep

- [ ] Repeated scene shapes: ______
- [ ] Repeated sentences or images: ______
- [ ] Repeated information across chapters: ______
- [ ] Repeated character beats (the same argument twice): ______

### Contradiction sweep

- [ ] Names, numbers, dates, distances checked against the ledger.
- [ ] Powers and limits checked against the rules ledger.
- [ ] Injuries, possessions, and locations checked.
- [ ] Deaths and witnesses checked.

### Thematic audit

- [ ] State the theme as a question: ______
- [ ] Identify two characters who embody rival answers.
- [ ] Identify the scenes where each pays a cost for their answer.
- [ ] Find any scene where a character states the theme. Either dramatise it or cut it.
- [ ] The final act answers the question through a choice, not a speech.

### Ending audit

- [ ] The final confrontation uses only established rules and resources.
- [ ] Costs are paid; the protagonist's final state reflects them.
- [ ] The final image rhymes with the opening and shows the change.
- [ ] The climax reuses at least one concrete element from the first chapter or first arc, with changed meaning.
- [ ] A reader can retell the causal chain without gaps.
- [ ] If the manuscript ends mid-scene (draft, serial, unfinished work), say so explicitly rather than treating it as an ending.

## Severity ranking

Rank findings 1-5 and fix in descending order. Do not fix 40 line-level issues while one act-level causality hole remains.
